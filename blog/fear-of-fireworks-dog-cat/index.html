Fear of fireworks in dogs and cats: getting through New Year’s Eve together

Published on 12 November 2026 · 5 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

Many dogs and cats are afraid of fireworks. What you can do weeks in advance, how to get through New Year’s Eve calmly and what you’d better not do.

For us it’s a party, for many animals it’s the scariest night of the year. Dogs and cats hear much better than we do, and the bangs come unexpectedly and from all sides. Trembling, panting, hiding or trying to run away: these are all signs of fear. Good preparation makes it a lot easier for your pet.

Start in time

Fireworks often start well before New Year’s Eve, so begin preparing a few weeks ahead. Was your pet very scared last year? Then see your vet in good time. There are medicines that help, but they need to suit your pet and sometimes have to be started early or tried out first. Never give your pet anything from your own medicine cabinet.

Make a safe place

  • Choose a quiet room, ideally without big windows, where your pet already feels at ease.
  • Put a basket or crate there with a blanket over it, so it becomes a den. Leave the door open: your pet should be able to choose.
  • Leave a few treats there for some days beforehand, so it becomes a nice place to be.

On the day itself

  • Walk your dog on the lead, well before it gets dark. Even a dog that never runs off can bolt in panic.
  • Keep cats indoors from the afternoon, with a litter tray inside.
  • Close windows, curtains and shutters, and put the radio or TV on softly.
  • Don’t leave your pet home alone if it’s scared.
  • Give dinner a little earlier, so your pet doesn’t have to eat just before the worst of the noise.
💡

Before New Year, check that the details linked to the microchip and on the tag are correct. If your pet does run away, you’ll get a call quickly.

Comforting is fine

People used to say you shouldn’t comfort a scared animal, because you would ‘reward’ the fear. That’s not true: fear isn’t a behaviour you teach with attention. Stay calm yourself, talk softly and let your pet come to you or hide as it likes. Never punish your pet for being afraid, and don’t pull it out of its hiding place.

Rabbits, guinea pigs and birds

Animals that live outside get frightened too. If you can, move the hutch into a shed or garage, or partly cover it with a blanket to make it darker and quieter. Keep enough ventilation and give extra hay to burrow into.

For the long term

Is your pet very scared every year? Then ask your vet or a behaviour specialist about training after New Year. Getting your pet used to sounds very gradually during quiet months can make the fear smaller. It takes time, so don’t wait until December.
