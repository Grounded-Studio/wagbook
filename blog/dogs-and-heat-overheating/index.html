Your dog and the heat: how to prevent overheating

Published on 12 November 2026 · 5 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

Dogs don’t cope well with heat. When it gets dangerous, which dogs are most at risk, how to recognise heatstroke and what to do straight away.

We sweat to cool down, but dogs can hardly do that. They lose heat mainly by panting. On warm days that soon isn’t enough, and a dog can overheat. That happens faster than many people think, and it can be life-threatening.

Never leave your dog in the car

Not ‘just for a minute’, not with a window open and not in the shade. A car in the sun quickly becomes tens of degrees hotter than outside, even on a day that doesn’t feel very hot. So take your dog with you, or leave him at home.

Which dogs are most at risk?

  • Dogs with a short nose, such as French bulldogs, pugs and English bulldogs. They can’t pant as well.
  • Puppies, older dogs and dogs with heart or lung problems.
  • Dogs that are overweight or have a thick coat.
  • Dogs that love to keep running and won’t stop by themselves.

Getting through warm days

  • Walk early in the morning and late in the evening, and keep it short in the middle of the day.
  • Feel the pavement: if you can’t keep your hand on it for seven seconds, it’s too hot for paws.
  • Always provide fresh water and shade, on the go too.
  • Don’t let your dog run alongside your bike when it’s warm.
  • A cooling mat, a wet towel to lie on or a paddling pool in the garden can help.

Recognise heatstroke

Watch for very fast, heavy panting, lots of drooling, a bright red or pale tongue and gums, restlessness, wobbling, vomiting or diarrhoea, drowsiness or collapse. With these signs, every minute counts.

What to do straight away

  • Move your dog to a cool, shady place.
  • Cool him with cool, not ice-cold, water. Start with the belly, paws and neck and keep pouring water over him.
  • Don’t leave a wet towel lying on his back: it can trap the heat.
  • Offer small sips of water if he wants to drink, but don’t force it.
  • Call your vet right away and say you’re on your way. Keep cooling during the trip.
💡

Even if your dog seems to recover: always have him checked. Heatstroke can still damage organs later.

What about other pets?

Cats, rabbits, guinea pigs and birds don’t cope well with heat either. Rabbits and guinea pigs already struggle at around 25 °C. Put hutches in the shade, give plenty of water and, for example, a frozen bottle of water wrapped in a cloth to lie against.
