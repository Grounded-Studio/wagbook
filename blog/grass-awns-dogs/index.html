Grass awns and your dog: small, but sometimes dangerous

Published on 14 November 2026 · 4 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

In spring and summer, grass awns can work their way into your dog’s ears, nose or between the toes. How to recognise it and how to prevent it.

Spring and summer are lovely for walks. But as soon as the grass grows tall and dries out, small troublemakers are waiting: grass awns, also called grass seeds or foxtails. They are the pointed seed heads of some grasses, with tiny barbs that only go one way. Once in the coat or skin, they keep working deeper.

Where do they end up?

  • Between the toes: a lump or swelling, and your dog keeps licking the same paw.
  • In the ear: suddenly lots of head shaking, a tilted head or scratching at the ear.
  • In the nose: violent sneezing, sometimes with a little blood, and pawing at the nose.
  • In the eye: squinting, watering or a red eye.

Prevention is easier

  • In spring and summer, avoid walking through tall, dry grass with seed heads.
  • Check paws, ears and armpits after a walk, especially in long-haired dogs.
  • Keep the hair between the toes and around the ears short.
  • Brush the coat after a walk through fields.
💡

See an awn still loose in the coat? Carefully pull it out. Is it already in the skin, ear or nose? Go to the vet and don’t try it yourself.

Why see the vet quickly?

A grass awn doesn’t go away by itself. Because of the barbs it moves deeper, which can cause an infection or an abscess. In the ear it can damage the eardrum. The sooner the vet removes it, the easier it usually is.
