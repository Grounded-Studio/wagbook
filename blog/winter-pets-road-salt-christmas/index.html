Winter with your pet: road salt, antifreeze and Christmas

Published on 14 November 2026 · 5 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

What to watch out for in winter for your dog, cat or rabbit: road salt, antifreeze, the cold and the dangers around Christmas.

Winter brings cosy days inside and fresh walks outside. Keep a few things in mind and your pet will get through the cold months well.

Road salt

Road salt can dry out and irritate your dog’s paw pads. When your dog licks its paws afterwards, it swallows salt too. Rinse the paws after a walk with lukewarm water and dry them well. A paw balm can help against cracks.

Antifreeze is very dangerous

Many antifreezes contain ethylene glycol. It tastes sweet, so dogs and cats like to lick it, and even a small amount can be fatal to the kidneys. Clean up spilled antifreeze right away and store it tightly closed. If you think your pet licked some, go to the vet immediately. Speed really matters here.

The cold

  • Old dogs, puppies and short-coated dogs get cold faster. A coat can help.
  • Don’t let your dog walk on thin ice.
  • Rabbits and guinea pigs living outside need a dry, draught-free hutch with extra straw. Check that the water isn’t frozen.

Christmas and New Year

  • Chocolate is toxic to dogs and cats; the darker, the more dangerous.
  • Xylitol (in sugar-free sweets and chewing gum) is very toxic to dogs.
  • Raisins and grapes, also in Christmas cake and stollen, can damage dogs’ kidneys.
  • Lilies in a Christmas bouquet are life-threatening for cats, even the pollen.
  • Tinsel and ribbon can get stuck in a cat’s gut.
  • Chicken or turkey bones can splinter. Don’t give them.
💡

Keep the food on the Christmas table out of reach and ask guests not to slip your pet anything.

Around New Year, fireworks play a big role. You can read more about that in our article on fear of fireworks.
