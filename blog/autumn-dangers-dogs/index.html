Autumn dangers for your dog: acorns, conkers, mushrooms and harvest mites

Published on 14 November 2026 · 4 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

Autumn is lovely for walks, but some things on the ground are better left alone. What’s dangerous for your dog and what to do if things go wrong?

Wet leaves, the smell of the woods and something to sniff everywhere: for many dogs autumn is a treat. But that’s exactly when there are things on the ground your dog had better leave alone.

Acorns

Acorns contain tannins that can irritate the stomach and gut. Your dog may vomit or get diarrhoea. If a dog eats a lot of them, the kidneys can suffer too. Green, unripe acorns are the riskiest. An acorn can also get stuck in the gut.

Horse chestnuts (conkers)

The shiny conkers from the park are not edible for dogs. They can cause vomiting, diarrhoea and tummy ache. A whole conker can also block the gut, especially in small dogs. So don’t let your dog play with them like a ball.

Mushrooms

Most mushrooms aren’t dangerous, but some are very toxic, such as certain fly agarics and death caps. Because you often can’t tell the difference: if your dog ate a mushroom, call your vet right away. If you can, bring a piece or a photo.

Harvest mites

Do you see tiny orange dots between the toes, around the eyes or on the ear edges, and is your dog scratching a lot? Those may be harvest mite larvae. They live in grass and bushes and cause intense itching. Your vet knows which treatment helps.

💡

Bring a tasty treat on autumn walks. If your dog comes back when you call, it’s easier to leave something on the ground.

What to do if your dog ate something wrong

  • Call your vet and say what, how much and when.
  • Don’t make your dog vomit yourself unless the vet tells you to.
  • Keep an eye on your dog: vomiting, diarrhoea, lethargy or trembling are reasons to go right away.
