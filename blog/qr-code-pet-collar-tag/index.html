A QR code on the collar: home again faster

Published on 15 November 2026 · 4 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

A microchip needs a scanner. A QR code on the tag lets anyone with a phone call you straight away. Here’s how to make one for free.

Your dog slips through the gate, or your cat doesn’t come home one night. Usually a neighbour or passer-by finds your pet first. How quickly they can reach you decides how quickly your buddy is back home.

A microchip matters, but it’s not enough

In many countries a microchip is required for dogs, and it’s a big help for cats too. But a chip can only be read with a special scanner, at the vet or a shelter. Someone who finds your pet in the street can’t do anything with it. Something on the collar that anyone can read right away saves hours.

Why a QR code?

  • Every phone can read it. Just point the camera at it, no app needed.
  • Call straight away. The finder gets your name and number as a contact card, with a call button.
  • Small and cheap. No engraving needed: print, cut out and stick.
  • New number? Simply print a new one.
💡

Don’t put your address on the tag. A phone number is enough, and it keeps you safe.

How to make one with Wagbook

  • Open your pet’s profile and tap QR tag.
  • Check your phone number and add your name if you like.
  • Tap Print stickers for a sheet with 40, 30 and 25 mm stickers, or share the code as an image.

Your number is inside the code itself. Nothing goes over the internet or through us, so the code keeps working even if the app ever leaves your phone.

Tips for the tag

  • Pick the biggest sticker that fits: the bigger, the easier to scan.
  • Put a layer of clear tape over it so it can handle rain and mud.
  • Test it with your own phone before it goes on the collar.
  • Cats: use a breakaway collar that springs open if it gets caught.
  • Keep your microchip registration up to date when you move or change number.
