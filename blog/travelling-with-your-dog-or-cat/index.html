Travelling with your dog or cat: what needs to be in order?

Published on 5 November 2026 · 5 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

Microchip, pet passport, rabies vaccination and the 21-day rule: how to prepare for a holiday in Europe with your dog or cat.

A holiday with your dog or cat is wonderful, but you don’t want surprises at the border. Within the European Union, the same basic rules apply to dogs, cats and ferrets. They aren’t difficult, but the order and the waiting time matter. So start well in advance.

1. A microchip, before the rabies shot

Your pet needs a microchip that your vet can read. The chip must be in place before the rabies vaccination is given. If the shot came before the chip, it doesn’t count for travel and has to be repeated.

2. A European pet passport

Your vet issues the blue European pet passport. It holds the microchip number, your details and the vaccinations with date, batch number and stamp. Always take the passport with you; a photo on your phone is a handy backup, but it is not a replacement.

3. Rabies vaccination and the 21-day rule

A valid rabies vaccination is required. Your pet must be at least 12 weeks old at the first shot. After that first vaccination you must wait 21 days before crossing the border. A booster given on time does not restart the waiting period. If the vaccination has expired, you start again with a first shot, including the wait.

💡

Count back from your departure date. Leaving in four weeks and no rabies vaccination yet? Then it needs to be done this week.

4. Extra rules per country

Some countries ask for more. Dogs travelling to Ireland, Finland, Malta or Norway need a tapeworm treatment given by a vet between 24 and 120 hours (1 to 5 days) before arrival, recorded in the passport. Outside the EU, and for the United Kingdom, different rules apply. Always check the official government website of your destination shortly before you leave.

5. Think about the trip itself

  • Bring medication for the whole holiday, plus a few extra days.
  • Look up a vet near your destination in advance.
  • Protect your pet against diseases that are more common there, such as leishmaniasis or heartworm in southern Europe. Ask your vet what is sensible.
  • Make sure your phone number on the tag or in the microchip registry is correct.
