Blue-green algae: when shouldn’t your dog swim?

Published on 14 November 2026 · 4 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

Blue-green algae can be very dangerous for dogs. How to recognise the water, how to see something’s wrong and how to act fast.

On a warm day, your dog wants nothing more than to jump in the water. But in summer, blue-green algae can grow in still water. They are actually bacteria (cyanobacteria) that can produce toxins. For dogs this can become serious quickly, because they swallow water and lick their coat clean.

Recognise the water

  • A green, blue-green or sometimes brown layer on the water, like paint or pea soup.
  • Green specks or streaks in the water.
  • Scum that gathers on the side the wind blows towards.
  • A musty or rotten smell.
💡

Many countries publish warnings for bathing water online. Even without a sign: if in doubt, keep your dog out.

Signs in your dog

Symptoms can start within minutes to hours. Watch for vomiting, diarrhoea, lots of drooling, trembling, wobbling, lethargy, breathing difficulty or seizures.

What to do straight away

  • Get your dog out of the water and don’t let it drink from it.
  • Rinse the coat well with clean water. Wear gloves yourself if you have them.
  • Call your vet or an emergency clinic right away, even if your dog doesn’t show anything yet.
  • If you can, take a photo of the water.

Prevention

  • On warm days, always bring fresh water, so your dog doesn’t need to drink from a puddle or pond.
  • Choose flowing water or official bathing water without a warning.
  • Rinse your dog after swimming.
