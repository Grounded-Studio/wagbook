Fleas and ticks: how often should you treat your dog or cat?

Published on 5 November 2026 · 4 min read · Lees in het Nederlands

How often to treat your pet against fleas and ticks, why winter counts too, and why you should never use a dog product on a cat.

Fleas and ticks are more than a nuisance. Fleas cause itching and can pass on tapeworm, and ticks can transmit diseases such as Lyme disease and babesiosis. A good schedule prevents a lot of trouble. But how often is ‘often enough’?

It depends on the product

There is no single answer, because each product lasts a different length of time. Roughly, there are three kinds:

  • Spot-on drops: usually every 4 weeks.
  • Tablets: there are tablets for 1 month and for 12 weeks.
  • Collars: often work for several months, as long as they stay on properly.

Always read the leaflet and follow your vet’s advice. Your vet knows which product suits your pet, its age, weight and lifestyle.

In winter too?

In north-western Europe, ticks are most active from spring to autumn, but in mild weather they are around in winter as well. Fleas survive indoors all year, in rugs, baskets and cracks. That’s why many vets recommend treating all year round, especially for dogs and cats that go outside.

💡

Treat all dogs and cats in the house at the same time. One untreated pet is enough to keep the fleas around.

Careful: dog products are dangerous for cats

Some flea and tick products for dogs contain permethrin. It is toxic to cats, even in small amounts. So never use a dog product on a cat, not even ‘just a little’. In doubt? Call your vet.

Found a tick?

  • Remove it as soon as possible with a tick hook or tick tweezers, close to the skin, with a calm twisting or pulling motion.
  • Don’t use alcohol, oil or other products to ‘stun’ the tick.
  • Keep an eye on the spot for a few weeks and see your vet if your pet gets ill, lethargic or feverish.
